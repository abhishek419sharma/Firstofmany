How this menu follows your rules
  • Gluten-free: rotis use jowar, bajra, ragi, makki, besan or rajgira. No wheat, maida, daliya or sooji. Check labels on masala blends, hing and sattu, as some contain wheat or barley.
  • No maida, no fried food, no added sugar: dishes are roasted, steamed, grilled or pressure-cooked with a light tadka. Sweetness comes only from whole fruit and a little soaked anjeer.
  • No red chilli: masalas use black pepper, ajwain, garam masala and dhania-jeera. Fresh green chilli is small and optional, so skip it if it upsets your stomach.
  • No soya anywhere in the menu.
  • Chicken and fish: only grilled, as a side at lunch and dinner, never in gravy. Eggs are at breakfast only, not in the side.
  • In season: raw mango, jamun, melons, plums and peaches only appear in their summer months, and fresh amla and sarson only in winter.
  • Every morning: soaked almonds, walnuts and anjeer, plus a warm digestive drink. Anjeer is a traditional help for sluggish bowels and has natural sugar, so the portion is small.
  • Buttermilk and flax daily: lunch always includes chaas with ground flax (alsi). Flax, chia, pumpkin and sesame seeds also appear in chutneys, drinks and snacks.
  • Breakfast always has 4 egg whites + 1 yolk and a collagen protein shake. Collagen lacks the amino acid tryptophan, so it counts toward your protein total but does not replace eggs, dal and dahi.
  • Light dinner: no dal or legumes (dals, rajma, chana, sprouts, besan), a smaller roti, and no rich or heavy dishes such as paneer gravies or egg curry. Those stay at lunch. Try to finish dinner 2–3 hours before bed.
  • Sluggish bowels: the planner favours fibre-rich fruit (papaya, guava, pear), flax and chia, soaked anjeer, greens and dahi, and starts the day with warm water drinks. Fibre works best with plenty of water. If the problem continues, or there is pain or blood, see a doctor.
